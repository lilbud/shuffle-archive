It’s hard to imagine in this age of splintered attention and media overload, but on July 13, 1985 nearly forty percent of the entire world’s population was glued to their screen watching a singular event.

It kicked off in London and continued in Philadelphia, two stages hosting virtually every major living rock artist, and quite a few minor ones too.

Live Aid went down in history as arguably the greatest concert event ever staged, and it started at Wembley Stadium with a band called Status Quo, a popular British band that hadn’t charted in America since 1968.

And yet the BBC insisted that the band open the show, which it planned to broadcast globally in its entirety.

Why kick off with a band unknown to much of its audience? Because of one specific song–a song that wasn’t even their own.

When John Fogerty recorded “Rockin’ All Over the World,” he was at a career low point. Credence Clearwater Revival had disbanded, and his first solo album flopped.

His self-titled follow-up fared better. “Rockin’ All Over the World” was the album’s lead single, and it notched a respectable #27 on the Billboard Hot 100 in the summer of 1975.

The song might have faded into the collective unconscious were it not for Status Quo, who recorded a heavier boogie-rock version in two years later. Their version went all the way to #3 on the UK charts and became a hit across of much of Europe as well.

[Watch on Youtube: Watch Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1cJGL9qEYUE)

By 1985, everyone knew the song, but almost no one knew it was a cover.

At least one person did, though.

Bruce Springsteen is a devoted lifelong Fogerty fan, often calling him the Hank Williams of his generation. So when he brought the River Tour to Europe for his first major arena tour there, he often closed his shows with a fitting Fogerty tribute (which most of his audience almost certainly attributed to Status Quo).

That first performance was faster than either Fogerty or Status Quo played it, but by the time he returned to Europe on the Born in the U.S.A. Tour, he’d slowed it down a bit.

[Watch on Youtube: Watch Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TSLi9GVDDvI)

“Rockin’ All Over the World” was already in heavy rotation in Springsteen’s setlists in the summer of 1985, but when Live Aid (an event from which Bruce was conspicuously absent) kicked off with it, the song became forever cemented as a timeless rock anthem, capturing the mission, scope, and euphoria of the event in a way no other song could have.

Something about that “I like it, I like it” chorus demands to be sung aloud. The Live Aid crowd picked up their cue just like Bruce’s audience did.

[Watch on Youtube: Watch Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eEO6v-YiS00)

From that point forward, “Rockin’ All Over the World” became an encore mainstay whenever Bruce toured Europe, even after he put the E Street Band on hiatus in the early nineties.

[Watch on Youtube: Watch Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fv4KHHPkZE)

“Rockin’ All Over the World” has been much rarer in the 21st century. But if you’re keen to hear Bruce perform it live, here’s a tip: make sure you’re at the closing show of a tour.

Starting with the Magic Tour, and all the way through the post-Covid era, Springsteen almost always closes out his tours with a final encore of “Rockin’ All Over the World.”
