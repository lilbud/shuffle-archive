“Girlfriend” or “Girl*fiend”?*

We may never know, because it’s too late to ask the songwriter, and the titular word never actually appears in the lyrics.

[Watch on Youtube: Watch Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIvAe-VAPoE)

Still, the mainstream consensus is that Slim Dunlap’s 1996 infectious album track had a typo in its original release, both on the album cover and the disc itself.

<img src='https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/R-3464998-1493976245-8085.jpg' alt='' title='' width='758' height='758' />

But that wouldn’t explain while it was still spelled that way on a 2005 re-issue with new album art.

Or on a 2013 tribute album on the same record label.

Or why Dunlap himself introduced the song as “Girlfiend” when he played it live.

[https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Slim-Dunlap-Band-Girlfiend.mp3?_=1](https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Slim-Dunlap-Band-Girlfiend.mp3?_=1)

<https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Slim-Dunlap-Band-Girlfiend.mp3>

Yet common wisdom says that was a typo, and Bruce Springsteen seems to be among the many who insist on inserting the missing “r” in the title, as he did when he revealed an until-then unknown studio cover of the song by the E Street Band during an E Street Radio appearance two days after Dunlap’s passing.

[https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Video-Project-1.mp3?_=2](https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Video-Project-1.mp3?_=2)

<https://estreetshuffle.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Video-Project-1.mp3>

Count me among the few who think Bruce is among the many who simply mis-read the title all these years.

It’s still a great cover of a great song by a powerful rock and roll voice who left us too soon.
